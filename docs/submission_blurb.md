# Submission text: Bundesbank / CEPR / LawFin conference

**Financial Stability in the Era of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Finance**  
Munich, 18–19 March 2027 · submission deadline 2 November 2026

**Title:** The Price of a Design Parameter: Bank Valuations in the Digital Euro Legislative Process  
**Author:** Philip Kroos

## Abstract for a submission form

The main financial-stability risk of a retail central bank digital currency is deposit flight, and the holding limit on the digital euro was designed to contain it. This paper provides a market test of whether investors regarded that risk as material once legislators began setting the instrument's parameters. I code 22 verified legislative and central-bank event dates between 2023 and 2026 by their documented design content and relate them to abnormal returns according to bank deposit exposure. A bank one standard deviation more deposit-funded gains 0.05 percentage points more on an event that limits the digital euro (permutation p = 0.45). Dating the rapporteur's draft report by its first press coverage gives −0.01, and aligning all events to their documented release times gives 0.03 to 0.05 (permutation p ≥ 0.61); every pooled specification excludes effects one third the size of those documented for 2020. Events that set a design parameter show no detectable valuation response. With predetermined exposure and strictly pre-event betas, the same design detects an effect of the magnitude documented in 2020 on the day the Eurosystem report appeared. A new dataset of 309 disclosed meetings shows that the same parameters were nonetheless intensely contested.

**One-line contribution:** a pre-specified bank-level market test of deposit-flight risk from CBDC design, with a positive control, informative magnitude bounds, and a new negotiation dataset.

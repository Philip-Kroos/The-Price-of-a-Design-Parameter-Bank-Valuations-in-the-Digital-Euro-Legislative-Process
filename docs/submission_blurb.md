# Submission text: Bundesbank / CEPR / LawFin conference

**Financial Stability in the Era of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Finance**  
Munich, 18–19 March 2027 · submission deadline 2 November 2026

**Title:** The Price of a Design Parameter: Bank Valuations in the Digital Euro Legislative Process  
**Author:** Philip Kroos

## Abstract for a submission form

The main financial-stability risk of a retail central bank digital currency is deposit flight, and the holding limit on the digital euro was designed to contain it. This paper provides a market test of whether investors regarded that risk as material once legislators began setting the instrument's parameters. I code 22 verified legislative and central-bank event dates between 2023 and 2026 by their documented design content and relate them to abnormal returns according to bank deposit exposure. When every event is dated by the day its content became public, the relative gain of a bank one standard deviation more deposit-funded, on an event that limits the digital euro, is −0.01 percentage points (placebo-date p = 0.91); the pre-specified dating gives 0.05 (p = 0.45). Allowing for shocks common to all banks on an event day, the estimates exclude effects about a quarter to a third the size of those documented for 2020. Events that set a design parameter show no detectable valuation response. With predetermined exposure and strictly pre-event betas, the same design finds a response of the magnitude documented in 2020 on the day the Eurosystem report appeared. A new dataset of 309 disclosed meetings shows that the same parameters were nonetheless intensely contested.

**One-line contribution:** a pre-specified bank-level market test of deposit-flight risk from CBDC design, with a positive control, informative magnitude bounds, and a new negotiation dataset.
